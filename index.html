Case studies

Five projects, in detail

People Operations built from zero

Ridecell | Dec 2018 - Jan 2026

Challenge

Ridecell had no documentation beyond outdated files, headcount lived in spreadsheets, and employee questions had no tracked channel.

What I did

Built a 20-25 SOP library with named owners and backups, ASKPeople on Jira and Confluence with tiered SLAs, and Sapling as the HR source of truth, integrated with ADP and connected to Greenhouse, Slack and Gmail through Zapier.

Result

6 consecutive clean SOC 2 Type 2 audits, 5 to 10 tickets a day answered in 24 to 48 hours, and approvals cut from 1 to 2 days to 2 to 3 hours.

What I would do differently

I'd have started the SOP library and ticketing system in the first months. Documenting as I went would have been easier than catching up later.

One approval path for every HRIS change

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

HRIS changes ran through an undocumented, unapproved process that created audit risk, and early feedback was that a form would be too process-heavy.

What I did

Built a Claude-powered Employee Action Form requiring documentation and approval before any HRIS change, plus Notion New Hire and Offboarding workflows with an owner on every task and automated Slack and email reminders.

Result

100% adoption within one to two months, an audit trail that had not existed, and preboarding triggered within 24 hours of offer acceptance.

What I would do differently

I'd have piloted the form with one team first. Some people said it was too process-heavy, and early proof from a small group might have won them over faster.

A reorganization with cost visibility

Prezent AI | Feb 2026 - Apr 2026

Challenge

A reorganization affecting 18 employees across the US and India needed accurate severance and COBRA numbers fast, and leaders had never seen the full cost.

What I did

Built AI-enabled severance and cost modeling with Claude and Gemini that calculated severance, COBRA cost and COBRA months owed and drafted the agreements, with every step tracked on a shared sheet for Payroll and IT.

Result

First-time RIF cost visibility and agreement prep cut from 20 to 30 minutes per employee to about 3 hours total.

What I would do differently

I'd have built the cost model before the reorganization was decided. Leaders could then have compared scenarios rather than just seeing the cost of the chosen one.

A plan for the first 90 days and beyond

A proposed plan built around the success picture in the role description.

Days 1 to 30

Listen and map

  • Meet People Ops, IT, Payroll, HR M&A and the HR Business Partners to learn how work flows today.
  • Map the current service model across Jira queues, Workday data and knowledge content.
  • Review ticket trends, SLA data and employee feedback to see where work piles up.
Days 31 to 60

Design the first moves

  • Pick two or three high-volume request types and write process maps, requirements and success metrics.
  • Decide which work routes to automation, self-service or a person, and write the operating procedures.
  • Review data quality and controls with the HR technology team before anything is built.
Days 61 to 90

Ship and measure

  • Launch the first AI-assisted workflow with a human approval step, in the Employee Action Form pattern.
  • Baseline speed, accuracy, cost and employee experience, and share the results with leaders.
  • Set a monthly planning rhythm for the next automation candidates.
By month 6

The outcome

  • A People Ops service model that anticipates needs and routes work to the right tier.
  • A team that uses data and generative AI as a daily decision-making tool.
  • Measurable gains in efficiency, cost, risk and employee experience.

This is a starting point. I would adjust it after the first conversations with the team and partner groups.
